\subsection{Description}\label{description}

This course is a comprehensive, language-agnostic treatment of
\texttt{Natural\ Language\ Processing} from foundations to modern
transformer architectures. It covers the linguistic concepts that
\texttt{make} text processing hard, the classical algorithms that
established the field, the statistical and neural methods that
revolutionized it, and the large-language-model era that defines its
current shape. Participants will study tokenization and morphology,
classical and distributional semantics, syntactic and dependency
parsing, language models from \texttt{n}-grams to transformers, sequence
labeling and tagging, text classification and sentiment analysis, named
entity recognition, machine translation, question answering,
summarization, dialogue systems, retrieval-augmented generation,
evaluation methodology, and the practical concerns of building NLP
systems in production. The course emphasizes the \emph{why} behind each
technique alongside the \emph{how}, equipping participants to choose the
right tool for the problem and to understand new techniques as they
emerge.

\subsection{Objectives}\label{objectives}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  understand the linguistic and statistical foundations that
  \texttt{make} text processing distinctive
\item
  apply tokenization, morphological analysis, and normalization to
  diverse languages and scripts
\item
  build and evaluate language models from \texttt{n}-grams through
  transformers
\item
  implement and reason about word and contextual embeddings
\item
  design and train text classifiers, including sentiment and topic
  models
\item
  perform sequence labeling for part-of-speech tagging and named entity
  recognition
\item
  parse syntactic and dependency structures with both classical and
  neural methods
\item
  apply attention and transformer architectures to standard NLP tasks
\item
  approach machine translation, summarization, question answering, and
  dialogue
\item
  deploy retrieval-augmented systems and evaluate their behavior
\item
  select appropriate evaluation metrics and recognize their limitations
\item
  navigate the practical concerns of building NLP systems at scale
\end{itemize}
